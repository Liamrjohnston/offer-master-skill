# Terms, claims and costs

## The terms card

Every guarantee should be writable as this card. If a line can't be filled, the guarantee isn't ready.

| Line | What to write |
|---|---|
| Who qualifies | Everyone who buys, or everyone who meets one or two conditions |
| The measure | How "it worked" is checked, agreed before the start (a before measurement, a log, a delivered item) |
| The time | When the result should happen, and the last day to claim |
| The remedy | Exactly what they get: an amount, hours or weeks |
| The cap | The ceiling on the remedy |
| How to claim | Who to contact, how, and what to show |
| What it doesn't cover | The results the owner can't control, said plainly |

Put the short version on the page and the card in the terms the buyer sees before they pay.

## The cost maths

Plan for a claim rate of one in ten. It's a planning assumption, not a prediction; label it (estimate). Then stress test at one in five.

- **Cost per 10 sales** = claims per 10 × cost of one claim.
- **Cost of one claim** = the refund amount, or extra hours × the owner's hourly rate (or cost), or the credit.
- **Share of revenue** = cost per 10 sales ÷ (10 × price).
- **Break even** = how many extra sales per 10 the guarantee must bring to pay for itself. Using price, that's cost per 10 ÷ price. Using profit per sale is more accurate when the owner gives it.

Example (fictional): price $800, remedy a full refund. Per 10 sales, 1 claim = $800, 10% of revenue (estimate). It pays for itself with 1 extra sale per 10 (estimate). At one in five: $1,600 per 10 sales, 20% (estimate).

If the owner can't afford the stress test, shrink the remedy (refund one month instead of the program, 4 extra hours instead of 10) rather than adding conditions.

## Handling claims

- Pay promptly and without argument when the conditions are met. A guarantee honoured quickly is good proof in itself.
- Ask every claimer what went wrong. Claims are the best feedback the offer gets.
- If claims run well above one in ten, the promise is too big or the first weeks don't deliver. Fix that, not the guarantee wording.

## Legal common sense (not legal advice)

- A guarantee adds to the buyer's legal rights; it can't take them away. Many places give buyers rights to refunds or cancellation for online and distance sales whatever the seller says. Never write "all sales final" or "no refunds" as if it overrides those rights.
- Say the terms before the buyer pays, in plain words, and honour them as written.
- Keep conditions reasonable. Conditions designed so nobody can qualify can be treated as misleading.
- In health, finance or legal fields, the owner should check wording with someone qualified.
