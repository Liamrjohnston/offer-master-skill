# Guarantee library

Ten types. Each has a pattern, when it fits, when it doesn't, and how to cap it. Examples are fictional.

## 1. No questions refund window

- **Pattern:** "Not for you in the first [14 or 30] days? Ask and get a full refund."
- **Fits:** the fear is fit or taste ("what if I don't like it?"); low delivery cost early on; courses, memberships, products.
- **Doesn't fit:** custom work used up straight away; anything where most of the value is delivered in the window.
- **Cap:** the window itself, and the price paid.
- **Example:** a recipe membership refunds anyone who asks in the first 14 days.

## 2. Do the work

- **Pattern:** "Do [two clear actions] for [time]. If we haven't delivered [the work we control], we [remedy]."
- **Fits:** the result depends on the buyer's effort (training, coaching, learning, habits).
- **Doesn't fit:** when the actions are hard to check, or you are about to guarantee weight, strength, income or bookings.
- **Rules:** the actions are the buyer's side and easy to show (sessions attended, check ins sent). The remedy is about the work the owner controls (the plan was delivered, the calls happened), not a body or money result.
- **Cap:** a refund of a set amount (one month, or the full price).
- **Example:** a running coach: "Do the 3 weekly runs and send your log each Sunday for 6 weeks. If we miss a planned session or don't deliver that week's plan, that week is refunded."

## 3. Delivery date

- **Pattern:** "If it isn't [delivered] by [date], you get [credit or refund] for each week late, up to [cap]."
- **Fits:** the fear is delays and being strung along; done for you projects with a clear finish.
- **Doesn't fit:** when the buyer controls the delays (missing content); write that in as the one condition.
- **Cap:** a maximum credit.
- **Example:** a kitchen fitter credits $100 for each week past the agreed finish date, up to $500, once the client has signed off the design.

## 4. Outcome

- **Pattern:** "If [measurable result] isn't [true] by [time], we [remedy, capped]."
- **Fits:** the result is mostly in the owner's control and measurable (a system works, a saving on a task they built, a report delivered).
- **Doesn't fit:** results that depend on markets, customers or the buyer's behaviour.
- **Cap:** a refund, or capped extra work.
- **Example:** a software trainer: "If your team can't run the monthly close in the new tool after our 3 sessions, we run a 4th and 5th session free."

## 5. Capped keep going

- **Pattern:** "Do [two actions]. Use it for [the same window as the proof]. If [the feared result] still isn't coming through the work, we [one extra piece of work], up to [cap]. This does not promise [the number], and it is not a refund."
- **Fits:** done-for-you work where the buyer says it won't pay off, or that someone cheaper could do it. A refund tied to their sales would be a promise the owner can't keep. Extra work answers the fear without that promise.
- **Doesn't fit:** with no cap. "Until it's fixed" can swallow a small business's month. Also doesn't fit as a refund of the fee because bookings, weight or income didn't happen.
- **Cap:** the hours or the one extra deliverable, written in the words. Any number you chose is (estimate) and goes in Check these.
- **Example:** a florist's site. Fear: a template would have been cheaper, and they don't believe orders will come. "Send your product photos by the date we agree and put the new link in your Google profile. Use it for 60 days. If orders still aren't coming through the site, we add one page and rework the listing, up to 6 hours, at no cost. This is not a promise of an order count, and it is not a refund." Cost: [your hourly rate] × 6 hours (estimate).

## 6. Partial refund

- **Pattern:** "If you stop, we refund any part not yet delivered."
- **Fits:** staged work and longer programs.
- **Doesn't fit:** when nearly all the value is in the first stage.
- **Cap:** the undelivered portion.

## 7. First phase

- **Pattern:** "Start with [phase one] for [smaller price]. If it doesn't [result], stop there and owe nothing more."
- **Fits:** high prices, cautious business buyers, new owners with thin proof. It lowers the first step instead of promising more.
- **Doesn't fit:** when phase one can't show anything meaningful on its own.
- **Cap:** the phase one price.
- **Example:** a marketing consultant sells a 2 week audit for a fifth of the full price, credited to the full project if they go ahead.

## 8. Service credit

- **Pattern:** "If we miss [standard], you get [credit] off your next bill."
- **Fits:** ongoing services with clear standards (response times, uptime, visits).
- **Cap:** a maximum credit per month.

## 9. Cancel any time

- **Pattern:** "Cancel any time from your account. No notice, no fees."
- **Fits:** subscriptions and memberships where the fear is being locked in.
- **Cap:** the current billing period.

## 10. Redo

- **Pattern:** "If it's not done to [the agreed standard], we come back and redo it free within [days]."
- **Fits:** trades and local services where quality is visible.
- **Cap:** one redo, within a set window.

## When no guarantee is right

Sometimes the honest answer is no guarantee: very custom work at a low margin, or a market where guarantees signal desperation. Then make Belief carry it with proof and a clear scope, and say plainly what happens if something goes wrong. Never write "all sales final" without checking the buyer's legal rights where the owner sells.

## Never

- Several guarantees stacked up. One clear one is stronger.
- Guarantees of weight, income, revenue, rankings or anything the owner can't influence.
- Double your money back or other bravado the owner couldn't pay.
- A guarantee the owner hopes nobody claims. Assume people will.
- A guarantee on the page that the owner doesn't actually honour.
