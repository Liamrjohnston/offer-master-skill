# Objection map

What buyers say is a symptom. The belief underneath is what the offer has to change. Use this map to decode, then change the offer itself; a clever reply to a fair objection doesn't sell anything.

## The six types

| Type | The buyer's inner sentence | Lever it hits |
|---|---|---|
| Value | "It's not worth this much to me." | Result, and how the price is shown |
| Trust | "It won't work for me." | Belief |
| Effort | "It'll take more work than I can give." | Ease |
| Time | "It's too slow" or "nothing changes if I wait." | Speed, and the cost of waiting |
| Identity | "This is for someone else, not people like me." | Result (in their words), Belief (proof from people like them) |
| Risk | "If it fails, I'm the one who loses." | Belief, the guarantee |

## The map

| They say | The belief behind it | Type | What to change in the offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I'll think about it" | "I'm not sure it works for someone like me, and the risk is mine" | Trust, risk | Proof from people like them, shown early. A guarantee on the main fear. A clear next step with a date |
| "Let me talk to my partner / team" | "I can't explain why this is worth it to someone else" | Value | A one line promise they can repeat, a short summary they can forward. Sometimes a real "ask your partner" is just a polite no: check what else they said |
| "It's too expensive" | "I can't see why it's worth this much" | Value | Show the price next to the cost of staying stuck. Make the result concrete. Offer a payment option. Don't discount |
| "It's more than I expected" | "I didn't know what it would cost, and nothing prepared me" | Value | Give a price range earlier, so the quote isn't a shock. Lead the quote with the result, not the line items |
| "I can't afford it right now" | Sometimes true. Often "it's not a priority" | Value, time | If true: a smaller first step or a payment plan. If not: the cost of waiting |
| "I can do it myself" | "I'm buying information, and information is free" | Value, effort | Sell what they can't do alone: the doing, the checking, the fixing, the accountability |
| "There's a free app / YouTube for that" | Same as above | Value, effort | Name what free can't give: someone who looks at their situation and corrects them |
| "My nephew / a friend can do it cheaper" | "A cheaper option looks the same to me" | Value | Show the difference in result, not features. Use proof of the result |
| "What exactly do I get?" | "I can't picture it, so I can't judge it" | Value, trust | Fixed scope, named parts, a timeline, what happens in week one |
| "How do I know it will work?" | "No proof I'll get my result" | Trust | Proof from people like them, a measurable promise, a guarantee |
| "How long until I see results?" | "It'll be ages before I feel anything" | Time | A week one win, progress shown along the way |
| "I don't have time for this" | "It'll eat my evenings" | Effort | Do more for them. Cut what they must do. Show the hours it actually takes |
| "I've tried things like this before" | "It failed me last time, so it'll fail again" | Trust, identity | Say what's different in how it works, not that it's better. Proof from people who'd also tried before, if the owner has it |
| "Not right now / maybe next month" | "Nothing changes if I wait" | Time | The cost of waiting. A real start date, if one exists |
| "Is this for beginners / for someone like me?" | "I'll be the odd one out" | Identity | Say exactly who it's for and who it's not for. Proof from people like them |
| "I'm not sure I'd stick with it" | "I'll quit, like before" | Effort, trust | Check ins, someone noticing when they slip, a first win to build on |
| "What if I don't like it?" | "I'm stuck with it if it's wrong" | Risk | A short no questions refund window, or a small first step |
| "Can I just buy one part?" | "I only want the bit I value; the rest is padding" | Value | Cut the padding. Maybe sell the valued part on its own |
| "Do you have reviews?" | "Nobody I trust has vouched for you" | Trust | Collect and show real reviews. Until then, lead with the owner's best named result and a guarantee |
| "Your competitor is cheaper" | "You're the same thing at a higher price" | Value | Show the difference in result and risk. If there isn't one, that's the real problem |
| "I need to see it working first" | "Show me before I pay" | Trust, risk | A paid first step, a demo, or a short pilot with a clear move to the full thing |
| "Send me more information" | Usually a polite exit | Value, trust | Send one page: promise, proof, price, next step. Then follow up once, on a date |
| Goes quiet after the quote | Price shock, or the quote doesn't remind them why | Value | A quote that opens with their problem and the result, a payment option, and one follow up within a few days |
| Books a call, doesn't show | "I'm not committed yet" | Trust, value | A reminder that restates the result; a page that does more selling before the call |
| Buys, then asks for a refund | "This isn't what I thought" | Product | Tighten the promise, fix week one, set expectations before they pay |
| Buys once, never again | "I got what I needed" or "nothing to do next" | Product | A clear next step after the first result |
| "It sounds great" (but doesn't buy) | "I like the idea; I don't see it for me yet" | Trust | The promise is too general. Make it specific to one buyer and one result |
| "Can you do it for less?" | "The price is a starting point" | Value | Hold the price. Offer less scope for less money, or a payment plan. Never the same thing for less |
| "Can you guarantee results?" | "I'm scared to waste the money" | Risk | A fair guarantee on what the owner controls. Never a guarantee on results the owner can't influence |
| "I'll wait until things calm down" | "I'm too busy to start" | Time, effort | Show how little it takes in week one. The cost of waiting |
| "Is this legit?" | "I don't know who you are" | Trust | Real identity, real clients, how to contact, terms in writing |

## Fair or unfair?

An objection is fair when it's true. Signs it's true: many buyers say it, it matches what the offer actually says, or it matches the owner's own doubts. When it's fair, change the offer. When it isn't (the buyer misunderstood), change the words so they can't misunderstand.

## Getting better objections

The owner's memory is a rough source. Better ones, from best to worst:

1. Exact words from lost buyers in writing (emails, messages after a quote).
2. Notes from sales calls, written the same day.
3. Asking recent buyers "what almost stopped you buying?"
4. Asking people who said no "what would have made it a yes?" One short message, no pressure.
5. The owner's guess.

Only the first four are evidence. If all you have is a guess, say so.

## For a brand new offer

There are no objections yet. List the five most likely ones for this buyer and price, mark each as a guess, and design the offer to answer the top two. Then make "write down every objection for the first month" part of the test.
