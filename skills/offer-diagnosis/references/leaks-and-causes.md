# Leaks and causes

## Path shapes and where each one leaks

### Content, sales page, call, sale (coaches, consultants, high price services)

| Where it drops | Most likely cause | What to fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Few reach the page | Traffic | Reach, content, asking people to look |
| Many reach the page, few book | Vague promise, hidden proof, risk on the buyer, unclear what happens on the call | Promise, proof near the top, guarantee, say what the call is for |
| Many book, few show up | Weak commitment | A reminder that restates the result; more selling before the call |
| They show up, few buy | Belief or risk on the call, price shock, no clear offer at the end | The offer itself, how the price is shown, a written recap after |

### Enquiry, quote, sale (trades, agencies, local services)

| Where it drops | Most likely cause | What to fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Few enquiries | Traffic, or a page that doesn't say who it's for | Reach, a clear "for [who]" line |
| Enquiries, then silence after the quote | Price shock, a bare price with no reminder of the result, no follow up | A quote that leads with the result and proof, a payment option, one follow up on a set day |
| "Too expensive" replies | Value not clear next to the price | Show the cost of staying stuck, show proof of the result, narrow the scope if needed |
| "A cheaper person can do it" | The buyer sees the same thing for less | Sell the result and the risk you remove, not the deliverable |

### Shop page, cart, checkout (products, courses, digital)

| Where it drops | Most likely cause | What to fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Views, few add to cart | Promise or proof on the page, unclear who it's for | Promise, proof, the one line |
| Cart, few check out | Surprise costs, trust at payment, too many steps | Show the full price early, payment options, a refund window |
| Buy, then refund | The promise was bigger than the product | Tighten the promise, fix the first experience |
| Buy once only | Nothing to continue into | A next step after the first result |

## The traffic test, worked

Sales = people at the top × every step rate after it, multiplied together. So a step that doubles doubles sales, no matter which step it is. That's why you can't find the leak by maths alone, and why the evidence (where people say no, and what they say) matters most.

The maths does answer one question well: is there enough traffic? Take the owner's goal. Double every step rate after the first. If they still fall short, traffic is part of the problem. Say so, and say the offer fix is still worth doing because it makes every future visitor worth more.

Example (illustration): 150 page views a month, 3 calls, 1 sale, and the owner wants 6 sales. Doubling both steps gives 4 sales (estimate). Short of 6, so too few people see it.

## Ranges and small numbers

- When the owner gives a range ("2 or 3"), use the midpoint and say so.
- Below about 20 buyers at a step, one buyer either way swings the rate a lot. Say the rate is rough. Don't chase a 1 percent change.
- Rates are always (estimate), even when the counts are (yours).

## "It used to sell": change the lightest layer first

When an offer that sold well has slowed, the core usually isn't broken. Work down this ladder and stop at the first layer that explains the drop:

1. **Where it's seen.** Did the traffic source change or shrink? Fewer people, not a worse offer.
2. **The words and pictures.** Same audience seeing the same post and page for months. Refresh the headline, the opening, the examples.
3. **The name and the angle.** Same offer, a fresh way in: a different buyer's problem first, a new proof story.
4. **The reason to buy now.** If the old reason ended (a season, a start date), find the next real one.
5. **The length or format.** Shorter, longer, group instead of one to one, or the reverse.
6. **The extras.** A new bonus that answers a new objection.
7. **How it's paid for.** One payment, a plan, a subscription.
8. **The core.** Only if all of the above don't explain it.

## Fixing problems that aren't the offer

Say these in one line in the card; don't turn the answer into a marketing plan.

| Problem | The one line fix |
|---|---|
| Traffic | "Post or ask more: the offer converts, too few see it." Name the channel already working, if any |
| No follow up | "Follow up once, two or three days after every quote, with a short reminder of the result." |
| Slow reply | "Reply to every enquiry the same day." |
| Calls with no clear close | "End every call with the offer, the price and a next step with a date." |
| Delivery problems | "Fix the first week before selling more; more buyers will mean more refunds." |
| Wrong buyer | "Say who it's for and who it isn't for at the top of the page." |
