# Test this month

One change, measured honestly, beats five changes nobody can read. The card always ends with exactly one test.

## The shape

"Change [one thing] for [time]. Watch [one number]. It's working if [threshold] (estimate)."

## Pick the change

Test the change that fixes the leak you named. In order of preference:

1. The fix for the top objection (a new promise, a first win, a guarantee).
2. The way the offer is presented at the leaking step (the quote email, the call close, the page headline).
3. Price or payment terms, only if the diagnosis pointed there.

Don't test two things at once. If the owner wants to change everything, pick the one most likely to move the leaking step and park the rest.

## Pick the number

The number at the leaking step, not the end result if volume is low.

| Leak | Watch |
|---|---|
| Page to enquiry | Enquiries per [N] page views |
| Enquiry or quote to reply | Replies per quote sent |
| Call to sale | Sales per call |
| Sale to finishing or renewing | Renewals, refunds, finishes |

## Set the threshold

Base it on the owner's current rate at that step.

- Current rate from their numbers, e.g. 1 in 6 quotes accepted.
- A threshold that would be a real change, e.g. 2 in the next 6, or 3 in the next 10. Label it (estimate).
- Say the time or the count: "for the next 10 quotes" is better than "for a month" when volume is low.

## Low numbers

Most small businesses will never have enough traffic for statistically clean tests. Be honest about that.

- Under about 20 at the step, results are noisy. Say "a signal, not proof".
- Count events, not percentages: "3 of the next 10" reads truer than "30%".
- Run the change on the next fixed batch (the next 10 enquiries), compare with the last 10 before it.
- Keep everything else the same during the test: same traffic source, same price, same follow up.
- Write down what buyers say. A change in objections ("too expensive" turning into "when can you start?") is evidence too.

## Forecasts

If you mention a likely effect, it's a rule of thumb, never a promise: one good change often lifts the step it targets by something like 10 to 40 percent, and changes multiply rather than add. Label it (estimate).

## Examples (fictional)

- "Send the new quote with the guarantee to the next 10 enquiries. Watch replies. It's working if 4 or more reply (estimate), against about 2 in 10 now."
- "Use the new headline for 4 weeks. Watch enquiries per 500 page views. It's working if they go from 3 to 5 or more (estimate)."
- "Offer the payment option on the next 8 calls. Watch sales. It's working if 3 or more buy (estimate)."
