# Edge cases

Use the normal stages unless one of these is true. Say which one you used, in the card's first lines.

## No numbers

They say it isn't selling and give no counts. Do not invent a funnel, a conversion rate or a leak percentage. Ask for the path (see it, enquire, buy) once. If they still don't have it, write the path as [placeholders], decode the objections they did give, and make "count the next 20 conversations" the test. A verdict of "it's the offer" needs some sign that people see it and don't buy. Without that, say you can't tell yet.

## Very low volume

One sale a month, or a handful of enquiries. Percentages lie here: 1 of 2 is not a rate you can plan on. Score the levers from the objections and the proof, and say the scores are thin. The test runs until a set number of conversations, not for a calendar week. Don't forecast a percent lift. "Change one thing and watch the next 20" is the honest test.

## It used to sell

Something changed. Walk the lightest layer first, in this order: traffic source, who the page speaks to, the promise, the price, the guarantee, then delivery. Change the first layer that lines up with what they changed. Don't rebuild the whole offer because a working one slowed down.

## They only want one piece

A guarantee, a name, a price, or a check of a page they paste. Call that one skill and answer in its format. Don't produce a full card they didn't ask for. If the piece they want would break a rule (a fake deadline, an uncapped guarantee), refuse that piece and give the honest version.

## The product is the problem

People buy, then quit, complain or ask for refunds. Say so in the first lines. The offer is not the main leak. Fix the first win and what happens in week one before you write more sales copy. You can still tidy the promise so it matches what is actually delivered.

## It's mostly traffic

Lots of the path is empty at the top: almost nobody sees it, and the few who do behave normally. Say that a better offer will not fix this on its own. Still do the offer if the page is vague, and put "get more of the right people to see it" next to the test, not instead of an honest offer.

## They want the price higher

You may recommend a higher price when the proof and the guarantee can carry it. Show the new price next to what the buyer gets and the cost of staying stuck. No "was $X" unless they actually charged $X. No crossed-out price, no "% off", no "price goes up Friday" unless that date and the new price are real and they asked you to use them.

## A capacity limit

"I can take 15" is the ceiling. It is not "3 spots left". Write [N] places open unless they told you how many are free today. If they are full, the honest line is a waitlist, not scarcity.

## Health, weight, income, or a business result with a time frame

Promise the work the owner controls: the plan, the calls, the pages, the invoice habit, the first workout. Past results stay past tense, attached to the named client, and not stretched to the next buyer. No "lose 9 kg in 12 weeks" as the offer. No "add $10k a month". If the only proof is that kind of result, show it as proof and keep the promise on the mechanism.

## Regulated or easy to overclaim

Supplements, medical outcomes, financial returns, legal results, job guarantees. Same rule, tighter. If a sentence would need a regulator's wording you don't have, cut it and put the question in Check these. The honesty check is not optional on these.

## They paste a page full of hype

Run `offer-honesty-check` on the paste before you rebuild. Cut invented spots, timers, bonus values and results that aren't in the brief. Then build from what survives. Tell them what you removed, in one line on the card.
