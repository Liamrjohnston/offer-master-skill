# The four levers

A buyer weighs four things against the price. Raise the first two, shrink the last two.

| Lever | The buyer's question |
|---|---|
| Result | "How much do I want what this gets me?" |
| Belief | "How sure am I that I'll actually get it?" |
| Speed | "How soon will I feel it working?" |
| Ease | "How much work, time and risk does it ask of me?" |

Score each one 1 to 10 against these levels. Give a one line reason with each score.

## Result
- 1 to 3: describes the activity ("coaching", "a website") not the outcome.
- 4 to 6: names an outcome, but a vague one ("get fit", "grow your business").
- 7 to 8: a specific outcome this buyer clearly wants, in their words.
- 9 to 10: specific, wanted, and tied to the bigger reason they care.

## Belief
- 1 to 3: no proof shown, no guarantee.
- 4 to 6: some proof, but generic or hidden (unnamed testimonials, proof buried at the bottom).
- 7 to 8: named results from people like the buyer, shown early.
- 9 to 10: strong named proof, a clear method, and a guarantee that puts risk on the seller.

## Speed (higher score means faster)
- 1 to 3: nothing felt for weeks.
- 4 to 6: a result promised at the end, little before.
- 7 to 8: a clear first win in the first week or two.
- 9 to 10: a visible win in the first days, and progress shown along the way.

## Ease (higher score means easier)
- 1 to 3: the buyer does almost everything themselves.
- 4 to 6: guidance is given, but the buyer still has to work most of it out.
- 7 to 8: done with them: plans made for them, regular checking and fixing.
- 9 to 10: done for them, or close to it, with very few decisions on their side.

## Using the scores

The lowest score is where most of the gain is. Fix that first, one or two changes at a time, so the owner can tell what worked.
