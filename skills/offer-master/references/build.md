# Building what's included

## Start from the obstacles

List what gets in the buyer's way:
- **Before buying:** doubts, confusion, fear of wasting money, not knowing where to start.
- **During:** getting stuck, losing motivation, not having time, mistakes.
- **After:** slipping back, not knowing what's next.

For each obstacle, write one thing the owner could do about it. Then keep only the things that are both (a) clearly valuable to the buyer and (b) cheap or quick for the owner to deliver. Drop the rest. An offer with fewer, sharper parts beats a long list.

## Don't add what the owner didn't offer

Every new deliverable, bonus or service you suggest is extra work for the owner. Mark each one (new) on the card and list it under "Check these" if it needs their time or money. Never present a new item as if it already exists.

## The first win

Design something the buyer gets or feels in the first seven days. A plan made for them, a first check in, a first fixed problem, a first measured result. Name it on the card. It answers "how soon will I feel it working?" and cuts refund requests.

## Describe outcomes, keep the facts

Rename parts by what they do for the buyer ("a plan built around your actual week") but never claim more than the part does. Don't promise "you won't slip back" when the part is a PDF.

## Bonuses

- Three to five at most. More looks like padding.
- Each one removes one named objection or obstacle. Say which.
- Time them: one that helps on day one, one for the hard middle, one for finishing or what comes after.
- A bonus is a real thing the owner will actually deliver. Never invent a product just to stack value.
- Don't hold bonuses back as pressure ("only if you buy today") unless that deadline is real.

## Talking about value

Keep two things separate:
- **What it's worth to the buyer:** time saved, money saved, problems avoided. Say this in their terms, labelled (estimate).
- **A stated price for an item:** only when there's a real comparable (the owner sells it separately, or a similar service has a known price). Total stated value should stay under twice the price. If there's no real comparable, give no price at all. That's fine.

Never write "you save $X" against a price the owner has not actually charged.
