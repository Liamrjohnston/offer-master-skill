# Honesty check

Run every line of the card against this list before you answer. Remove or fix anything that fails.

## Claims
- Every result, client, number and testimonial came from the owner. If not, cut it or use a [placeholder].
- Past results are described as what that client got ("Mark, 44, lost 9 kg in 12 weeks"), not as what every buyer will get.
- If you feature an unusually good result, add what's typical, or say results vary with effort.
- No statistic from "studies" or "research" unless the owner supplied a source.
- One client's result is proof for that kind of client and job. Don't promise it to other types of buyer or a bigger scope.
- Don't sharpen the owner's claims ("no missed appointments", "replies within the hour") unless they said so.

## Prices and value
- No "worth $X" without a real comparable price.
- No crossed-out or "was" price the owner hasn't genuinely charged for a fair period. Many countries (including the US, Canada, the UK and Australia) treat fake regular prices as misleading advertising.
- If a payment plan costs more in total, say so.

## Urgency
- Only real limits the owner enforces. No fake timers, fake spots, or rolling "ends tonight" deadlines.

## Promises
- No guarantee of health, weight, income or business results with a time frame.
- Guarantees list clear conditions and how to claim.

## Labels
Every number on the card ends with (yours) only if the owner wrote that exact number, or (estimate) if you worked it out, even when you worked it out from their figures.

This is common sense, not legal advice. If the owner is in a regulated field (health, finance, legal), tell them to check their wording with someone qualified.
