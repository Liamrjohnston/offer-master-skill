# Lever levels, in detail

Score each lever from the buyer's seat, looking at what they see today. Use the level whose description fits best; if it sits between two, take the lower one.

## Result: how much do they want what this gets them?

| Score | Definition | Service example | Course or product example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Names the activity only | "Bookkeeping services" | "A video course on baking" |
| 3 | Names a category of outcome nobody asked for | "Financial clarity" | "Become a better baker" |
| 4 to 5 | Names a real outcome, vaguely | "Get your books in order" | "Bake bread at home" |
| 6 | A real outcome with one specific detail | "Books ready for your accountant" | "Bake a proper sourdough loaf" |
| 7 to 8 | Specific and clearly wanted, in the buyer's words | "Books done every month so tax time takes an afternoon, not a weekend" | "A crusty sourdough loaf you'd be proud to put on the table, from your own kitchen" |
| 9 to 10 | All of that, tied to why they care | "...so you stop dreading April and know what you can spend" | "...the loaf your family asks for every Sunday" |

Signs of a low Result score: the page talks about the seller ("I'm passionate about..."), lists features first, or uses the seller's jargon.

## Belief: how sure are they they'll get it?

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | No proof, no method, no guarantee. "Trust me" |
| 3 | Some proof exists, but the buyer can't see it on the page or in the pitch |
| 4 to 5 | Generic proof: unnamed testimonials, "happy clients", star ratings with no detail, proof buried at the bottom |
| 6 | Named proof, but from buyers unlike this one, or shown late |
| 7 to 8 | Named, specific results from people like the buyer, shown early, with a clear method |
| 9 to 10 | All of that, plus a guarantee that puts the risk on the seller and several proof types (results, reviews, the owner's own story, a demo) |

Proof strength, strongest first: results the buyer can see for themselves (a demo, a trial, a before and after they can check), named results from people like them, named results from other people, third party mentions, the seller's own claims.

Belief is the most common low score in offers that don't sell, and the cheapest to fix when the proof already exists.

## Speed: how soon will they feel it working? (higher means faster)

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Nothing happens for months; the result arrives at the very end, if at all |
| 3 | Weeks before anything is felt; nothing named before the end |
| 4 to 5 | A result promised at the end, with a vague start ("we'll get going") |
| 6 | A first step named, but it's admin (an intake form, a welcome email) |
| 7 to 8 | A named first win the buyer feels in the first week or two |
| 9 to 10 | A visible win in the first days, then progress they can see along the way |

A first win must be something the buyer feels or uses, not a task they complete for the seller.

## Ease: how much does it ask of them? (higher means easier)

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | The buyer gets information and does everything alone |
| 3 | Information plus a community or forum, but no one looks at their work |
| 4 to 5 | Guidance given, but the buyer still works most of it out and makes most decisions |
| 6 | Some things done for them (templates, a plan), the rest on them |
| 7 to 8 | Done with them: a plan made for them, regular checking and fixing |
| 9 to 10 | Done for them, or close, with few decisions and little time on their side |

Ease also covers risk and hassle: long contracts, hard cancellations, confusing next steps and surprise costs all lower it.

## Common scoring mistakes

- Scoring what the owner could show rather than what the buyer sees.
- Giving Belief a high score for "lots of clients" when none are named.
- Counting a deliverable (a PDF, a call) as Speed. Speed is when the buyer feels a result.
- Scoring Ease high for done for you work when the buyer still has to supply content, decisions and approvals that stall it.
- Averaging the four. Don't.
