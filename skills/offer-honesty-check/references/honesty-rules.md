# Honesty rules, in depth

Why each rule exists, the edge cases, and fixed examples. All examples are fictional.

## 1. No invented numbers

A number reads as fact. If nobody gave it, it's made up, however reasonable it looks. Common offenders: "saves 10 hours a week", "3x more bookings", "92% of clients", "most people see results in 30 days".

- A sum worked out from the owner's numbers is fine, labelled (estimate).
- A planning assumption (a 1 in 10 claim rate) is fine, labelled (estimate) and named as an assumption.
- A guessed input inside a sum is not fine. Use a [placeholder]: "each month costs [hours lost] × [your rate]".

## 2. Label every number

On the offer card, every number ends with (yours) or (estimate).

- (yours): the owner wrote that exact number. "$1,200 (yours)".
- (estimate): anything worked out, even from the owner's figures. Doubling their "6 a month" to "12" is an estimate.
- Ranges: if the owner said "2 or 3 a month", 2.5 is an estimate; "2 or 3" is theirs.

On the page copy the owner will paste, labels would look odd. Keep the copy clean, use only the owner's numbers in it, and put a "Numbers on this page" line under it that labels each one.

## 3. Show the sum

An estimate that hides its inputs can't be checked. Write: "6 hours at $60 = $360 (estimate)". If an input is missing: "[hours] × [your rate]", and put the missing input in "Check these".

## 4. No fake scarcity

Scarcity is only honest if it's true, enforced and checkable.

- "I take 6 clients a month" (the owner's capacity) is not "only 2 left". Write "[N] places open" unless the owner said how many are free.
- A deadline must be real and kept. A timer that resets is a lie.
- "Open all year" is a fine answer.

## 5. No fake values

"Bonus: Starter Checklist (worth $197)" invites the question "who paid $197 for it?" If nobody has, it's a fake value. Allowed only when the owner sells that item separately at that price, or it has a real, checkable market price. Otherwise describe what it does: "A checklist so you don't miss a step in week one."

A "total value" built from invented item prices is the same problem, multiplied.

## 6. No fake anchors

A crossed out price, a "regular price" or a "was $X" must be a price the owner really charged, for a fair period, to real buyers. Many countries (including Canada, the US, the UK and Australia) treat fake regular prices and inflated "you save" claims as misleading. Honest comparisons: the owner's own higher option, the real cost of hiring someone, or the real cost of the problem.

## 7. Promise only what the owner controls

The owner controls the work, the delivery date, the number of sessions, the response time, the first step. They don't control a buyer's weight, health, income, revenue or bookings.

| Fails | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "Lose 8 kg in 10 weeks" | "A plan built around your week, checked every Sunday. [Client] lost [what they lost] in [time]." |
| "Double your revenue in 90 days" | "We set up [the work] in 30 days. Here's what [client] saw afterwards." |
| "Guaranteed results" | "If [the controllable thing] isn't done by [date], [capped remedy]." |

Past results are fine as proof: "Ana went from 4 to 15 bookings a month after launch." Not fine as a promise to everyone.

If an unusually good result is featured, say what's more typical, or that results depend on effort and situation.

## 8. No stretched proof

One result proves something for that kind of client, that job and that scope.

- A result from one café is proof for cafés, not "any small business".
- "7 sites built" is not "7 clinic sites" unless the owner said so.
- "Replies within a day" is not "replies within the hour".
- "Most clients" needs most clients; one client is "one client".
- "In the first 60 days after launch" stays exactly that; don't round it to "in 2 months, guaranteed".

## 9. No borrowed authority

"Studies show", "experts agree", "the industry average is", "most businesses lose" need a source the owner can name. If there isn't one, cut it. Industry benchmarks also don't diagnose this owner's offer; their own numbers do.

## 10. Mark what's new

Anything the owner doesn't currently offer is a suggestion. (new) after it stops the owner accidentally advertising something they don't deliver yet.

## 11. Honest plans and terms

- A payment plan that costs more must show its total in the same sentence.
- A guarantee needs its conditions (two at most), its cap, its time limit and how to claim.
- "Risk free" is only fair if the guarantee really removes the risk; usually "backed by [the guarantee]" is more accurate.

## 12. No made up voices

No invented testimonials, ratings, reviews, client counts, logos or "as seen in". Leave a slot: "[testimonial from a real client, with their permission]".

## Regulated fields

Health, money, legal advice, and anything aimed at children have stricter rules almost everywhere. Add one line telling the owner to check wording with someone qualified. Don't give legal advice.
