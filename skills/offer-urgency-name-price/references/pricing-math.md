# Pricing maths and rules

## Two different numbers, never mixed up

1. **What the result is worth to the buyer.** Worked out from the buyer's or owner's real numbers: hours saved × their rate, extra customers × what a customer is worth, costs avoided. Label it (estimate). If an input is missing, write a formula with a [placeholder]. This is what the price is compared with.
2. **A stated price for an item.** Only when there's a real comparable: the owner sells it separately, or the same thing has a known, checkable market price. Most items should carry no stated price.

## Cost of waiting

Cost of waiting per month = what the problem costs each month, from real inputs.

| The problem | The sum | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| Lost time | hours a month × hourly value | 10 hours × [your rate] |
| Lost customers | customers missed a month × value of one | [missed bookings] × [value of a booking] |
| Wasted spend | money spent on the thing that doesn't work | $300 a month on ads that don't convert (owner's number) |
| Getting worse | what grows if left alone | Weeds take another season; debt grows by interest |

Then: "Waiting 3 months costs about [3 × monthly] (estimate)." And, if useful: "The price is covered after about [price ÷ monthly] months (estimate)."

Never invent the monthly figure. A formula with [placeholders] is honest; a guessed number is not.

## Value to price

When the result is worth something measurable, the price should be a clear fraction of it. Show the ratio only from real inputs: "At [hours saved] a month, the $2,400 is covered in about [n] months (estimate)." Don't claim a ratio you can't show.

## Payment options

| Option | When it helps | Honesty rule |
|---|---|---|
| Pay in full | Default | None |
| Split in 2 or 3 | Big one off prices, project work | If the total is the same, say "same total". If more, say the total |
| Deposit then balance | Projects with a clear finish | Say when the balance is due |
| Monthly for a fixed term | Programs with a set length | State the term and the total ("3 payments of $250, $750 in total") |
| Monthly, cancel any time | Ongoing services | Say how to cancel |

- Offer a payment option before any discount.
- If the plan costs more in total, say so in the same sentence as the instalment. "4 payments of $260 ($1,040 in total, $40 more than paying in full)."
- Never describe the pay in full price as a saving unless the plan really costs more, and say both totals.

## Anchors

An anchor is fine when it's real: the owner's own higher option, the real cost of hiring someone to do it, or the real cost of the problem. An anchor is not fine when it's made up: a "regular price" never charged, a "total value" built from invented item prices, a crossed out number. Many countries (including Canada, the US, the UK and Australia) treat fake regular prices and "you save" claims as misleading advertising.

## Discounts

- Never to win a new customer. It teaches buyers to wait and draws the ones who leave first.
- Fine for existing customers (renewals, referrals), for a real season the owner enforces, or for a smaller scope at a smaller price.
- "Can you do it cheaper?" Offer less for less, or a payment plan. Never the same thing for less.

## Raising the price

Check all four first:

1. Documented results, with names and numbers.
2. The result is clear in the buyer's money or time.
3. The new price is still a clear fraction of what it's worth to them.
4. Result and Belief both score 7 or more.

If any is missing, fix that first. When raising: new buyers first, tell existing customers well ahead, and give them a fair option.

## Price signals

- "That's cheap" from buyers often means they don't trust it. Add proof, not discounts.
- "That's expensive" often means they can't see the value. Show the cost of staying stuck and the result.
- Prices ending in 7 or 9 are a minor presentation choice, not a fix for an offer that doesn't sell.

## Options and tiers

Same core result at every level. Higher prices buy more speed, more ease and more attention, not more information. Don't add a fake middle option to steer people.
