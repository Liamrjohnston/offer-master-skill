# Obstacles, what's included and what to cut

## Listing obstacles

Start with what buyers actually said. Then add what nearly every buyer of this kind runs into. Write each one in the buyer's voice.

| Kind | Questions to ask yourself | Example (fictional, a home organising service) |
|---|---|---|
| Knowing | What don't they know how to do? | "I don't know where to start" |
| Doing | What can they not do well, even knowing how? | "I start a room and never finish" |
| Their situation | What in their life gets in the way? | "I have two kids and no weekends" |
| Their head | What doubts or feelings stop them? | "I'm embarrassed for anyone to see the mess" |

Group them by when they hit: before buying, during, after. Keep the list to about fifteen; the top five matter most.

## Turning obstacles into parts

For each obstacle, write one thing the owner could do. Then pick the delivery level:

| Level | What it means | Raises Ease | Costs the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alone | The buyer gets a guide, template or recording | A little | Little, once |
| With them | The owner checks, corrects, plans with them | A lot | Time per buyer |
| For them | The owner does it | Most | The most time per buyer |

A useful way to price options: same core result at every level, with higher prices buying more speed, ease and attention, not more information.

## The keep, move, cut table

Score every part for value to this buyer (high, medium, low) and cost to the owner per buyer (low, medium, high), using the owner's real hours and costs where they gave them.

| Value | Cost | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| High | Low or medium | Keep in the core |
| High | High | Keep only if it makes the offer different; otherwise put it in a higher priced option |
| Medium | Low | Move to bonuses, if it kills a doubt |
| Medium | High | Cut |
| Low | Any | Cut |

Signs a part is padding: nobody mentions it when they buy, it duplicates another part, it's there to make the list longer, or it's "information" in an offer whose buyers want help.

## Parts that usually earn their place

- Something that gets the buyer started in the first days.
- A way to get unstuck between sessions (a message line with a reply time the owner can keep).
- A check that catches problems early.
- A clear next step at the end.

## Parts that usually don't

- Large PDFs and libraries buyers never open.
- "Lifetime access" to something they'll use once.
- A community with nobody in it.
- Anything that adds homework for buyers who already worry about time.

## Who it's not for

One line saying who shouldn't buy makes everything else more believable and saves the owner bad fits. "Not for people who want a meal plan without changing what they cook."
