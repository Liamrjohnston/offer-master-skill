# The one line promise and the week one win

## What a strong promise does

A buyer reads it once and knows who it's for, what they'll have at the end, roughly when, and why this isn't the free option. They could repeat it to a friend without looking.

## Shapes that work

| Shape | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|
| Result, time, how | "Your books done every month, so tax time takes an afternoon, with a bookkeeper who answers within a day." |
| Pain removed | "Stop losing Saturdays to your accounts. We do the books, you get a one page summary every month." |
| What free can't do | "Not another budgeting app: a real person who reconciles your accounts and flags problems before your accountant does." |
| Who, then result | "For café owners who hate spreadsheets: monthly books done for you, and tax time sorted in one meeting." |
| End state | "Open your laptop on the 5th of the month and your numbers are already there, checked." |

## Making it stronger without making it bigger

- **Concrete nouns beat abstract ones.** "A loaf you'd put on the table" beats "baking confidence".
- **Use the buyer's words.** If they say "I'm drowning in receipts", the promise mentions receipts.
- **Name the dread.** What they're scared of or sick of, said plainly.
- **Name the how in plain words.** "A tutor you talk to every week" beats "our proven conversational methodology".
- **Cut adjectives that aren't facts.** "Amazing", "transformational", "ultimate" add nothing and read as hype.

## The boundary

| Fine (the owner controls it) | Not fine (the owner can't promise it) |
|---|---|
| "A plan built around your week, in 48 hours" | "Lose 10 kg in 12 weeks" |
| "Your site live in 3 weeks, booking button tested" | "Double your bookings" |
| "Built to save your team a day of reporting a month" plus a guarantee on a measured saving | "Save 20 hours a week" with no proof |
| "Every lesson spent talking, not on worksheets" | "Fluent in 10 weeks" |

Results the owner has seen belong in the proof line, named and described as what that client got.

## Before and after (illustrations, fictional businesses)

| Before | Why it's weak | After |
|---|---|---|
| "Professional garden design services" | The activity, no outcome, no buyer | "A garden plan you can actually build, drawn for your yard and your budget, in two weeks" |
| "Unlock your potential with our coaching" | Abstract, no time, no buyer | "For new managers: run your first hard conversation well, with a coach who's in your corner every week for 8 weeks" |
| "Learn to code online" | Vague; the free option looks the same | "Not another tutorial: build and ship one real app in 12 weeks, with a mentor reviewing your code every week" |
| "Bookkeeping from $200/month" | Price first, no result | "Your books done every month and a one page summary by the 5th, so you always know what you can spend" |

## The week one win

It answers "how soon will I feel this working?" and is the single best defence against early refunds.

**Tests:** the buyer feels it or uses it; the owner controls it; it happens in the first seven days; it's named in one line.

| Business type | Week one win patterns |
|---|---|
| Coaching, training | A plan built for them; a first session that fixes one thing; a measured starting point they'll compare against |
| Done for you services | A first piece delivered (a draft, a mock up, an audit with one fix made); a call that ends with a dated plan |
| Courses | One finished small project in the first lesson; a template filled in for their situation |
| Local services | A first visit that solves the most visible problem; a written plan and fixed quote on day one |
| Products | Setup done in minutes; the first use gives a visible result |

Weak wins to avoid: "welcome pack", "onboarding form", "access to the portal". Those are admin, not wins.
