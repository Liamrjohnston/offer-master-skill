# Bonus patterns

A bonus is a small, real extra that kills one named doubt. That's the whole job. A bonus that doesn't answer a doubt is padding, and padding makes the offer look like it needs propping up.

## The three tests

All three must pass:

1. **It kills a named doubt.** You can write the objection it answers, in the buyer's words.
2. **It helps the core result happen.** It makes the main promise more likely, not a separate thing.
3. **It's cheap or quick to deliver.** Little or no extra time per buyer.

## Patterns by the doubt they kill

| The doubt | Bonus pattern | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| "I won't know where to start" | A start fast kit: the first step done or laid out for them | A cleaner's "first visit checklist" so the client knows what to clear before day one |
| "I'll get stuck and quit" | Stuck help: a way to get unstuck between sessions | A tutor's midweek voice note check in |
| "Will it work for someone like me?" | A fit check before they pay | A garden designer's 15 minute yard review call |
| "I don't have time" | Time saver: something done for them | A bookkeeper sets up bank feeds for them |
| "What if it fades after?" | A keep it going plan | A trainer's 4 week plan for after the program ends |
| "I can't decide which option" | Decision help | A short guide that tells them which option fits, honestly |
| "What will my partner / boss say?" | Something they can forward | A one page summary of what they get and what it costs |

## Timing

- **Day one:** a bonus that gets them moving fast.
- **The hard middle:** a bonus that catches people when they stall.
- **The end:** a bonus that keeps the result or points to what's next.

## How many

Three to five at the very most. One or two strong ones usually beat five weak ones. "No bonuses, the core answers the objections" is a perfectly good answer, and often the most honest.

## Value and prices

- Most bonuses should carry no price at all.
- Only show a price if the owner sells that exact thing separately, or there's a real, checkable price for the same thing elsewhere. Round down.
- Total stated bonus value should stay under twice the main price. If it's higher, the bonuses are doing the selling and the core is weak.
- Never write "worth $X" for something the owner made up, and never "you save $X" against a price never charged.

## Bad bonuses

- A product invented to stack up value ("Mindset Mastery Vault, $997 value").
- Anything that adds homework for buyers who worry about time.
- Bonuses that cost the owner real hours per buyer they haven't agreed to.
- "Only if you buy today" when that isn't true.
- A bonus that answers a doubt nobody has.

## Order on the page

Put the bonus right after the part it supports, or list bonuses in the order the doubts appear (start, middle, end). Don't open with bonuses; open with the promise and proof.
